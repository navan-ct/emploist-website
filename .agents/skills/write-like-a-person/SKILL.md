# Write like a person, not a model

Write like a person who ships the product, not a model summarizing the internet. Point first. Then the one fact that proves it. Read the draft aloud. If a sentence sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

These rules apply everywhere: titles, steps, captions, image overlay text, blog posts, help articles, UI copy.

## Punctuation

Never use an em dash (`—`) or en dash (`–`). Where a model would dash, split into two sentences, or join with a comma and `and` / `but`. Don't swap in a semicolon to keep the same rhythm. Use a period, comma, colon (lists only), or parentheses. Ranges: `1 to 6` or `1-6`.

Use straight quotes (`'` and `"`), never curly ones, in anything that gets published.

## Rhythm

- Mix short lines (under 8 words) with longer ones. Don't stack three sentences of the same length.
- Don't open a section with a grand "in today's world" setup.
- Don't close with a recap or a pep talk. End on a concrete next action.

## Banned words

delve, tapestry, navigate (figurative), landscape (figurative), realm, underscore, bolster, foster, harness, leverage, unpack, shed light, pave the way, pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, game-changing, innovative, robust, comprehensive, seamless, intricate, nuanced (as empty praise), vibrant, multifaceted, holistic, testament, unlock, tidy, dive in, elevate, utilize, showcase, meticulous, dynamic (as empty praise).

## Banned phrases

"it's worth noting", "when it comes to", "at its core", "at the end of the day", "plays a crucial role", "it cannot be overstated", "this is where X comes in", "let's break it down", "need a hand?", "fast path", "the other way around", "stay responsible", "one of the most important", "in today's … world/landscape", "needless to say", "ultimately", "that said", "I hope this helps", "in conclusion".

## Banned structures

- "It's not just X, it's Y." / "Not only X, but Y." / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." / "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- Rule-of-three stacks (tricolons) more than once per piece.
- The "**Bold term:** explanation sentence" list.
- Signposting ("Let's explore", "Now let's turn to").
- Restating the reader's question before answering.
- Sandwich intros that preview the whole piece.
- Decorative emoji (one per line, rockets, checkmarks).
- Medium-length sentences stacked evenly for a whole section.
- Slogan couplets: two short sentences posing as a clever rule ("Preview is for you. Publish is for guests."). Write one complete, warm thought instead ("Preview is for checking your work. Guests need the published link.").

## Do

- Use contractions.
- Specific names, URLs and counts beat adjectives.
- Don't hedge with empty "significant" or "important".
- No comparison marketing ("better than X", "no paywall like Y") unless the piece is explicitly a comparison.

## Editing pass

1. Search the draft for `—`, `–`, curly quotes and every banned word; fix each hit.
2. Read it aloud. Rewrite anything that sounds like a press release or a slogan.
3. Check the opening: does the first sentence make the point?
4. Check the ending: does it end on something the reader can do?
